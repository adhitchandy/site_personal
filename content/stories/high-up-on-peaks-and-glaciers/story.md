## Journey

With a small group singing at midnight, Adhit's spontaneous birthday party came to an end. In less than 24 hours, we were supposed to be on a train heading toward the Alps. It almost felt like a gift for both of us. But first, we needed to sleep and make the final preparations. Fortunately, due to a train cancellation, we were able to catch a later one. This allowed for a spontaneous facial treatment with Rachel. Around 9:30 p.m., we hurried to the train, and our adventure could finally begin.

We made ourselves comfortable on the train, learning a few Malayalam words and planning the trip. New passengers boarded during the night, chatting loudly, but they soon quieted down, and the train, along with our dreams, continued to roll on. We reached Munich station around 7:00 a.m. and then took a bus through Salzburg to the starting point of our hike.

## Day 1 – Along the Gosaukamm

We arrived at Vorderer Gosausee around noon, and the view was breathtaking, magical, and exhilarating. Adhit immediately took out his tripod and started taking photos. The experience caught the attention of others, who also asked for photos. Meanwhile, I enjoyed a refreshing dip in the lake – although my Garmin watch didn’t survive the swim.

After this long break, we were ready for the ascent. We didn’t realize at first that this would push us out of our comfort zone. Initially, we climbed the first steps at 920 meters with joy and excitement. The lake grew smaller and smaller, but the climb seemed endless. By late afternoon, we reached a small hut, knowing we still had nearly twice the elevation to go. The next landmark was supposed to be a chapel, but no matter where we looked, it didn’t appear. We continued with solemn faces, aware of the distance ahead.

Just then, a hiker passed by and told us about her hike, offering encouraging directions. She said the pass wasn’t far, and after crossing it, we would be able to see the hut from a distance. Her words gave us the energy we needed, and we pushed on.

We reached the Gosaukamm and continued. The monotonous view of stones began to wear on us, so Adhit started singing: "thithithara thi thi thai, thithai thaka thai thai thom." Step by step, we followed the red trail markers. The ascents grew tougher, and we increasingly relied on our hands for support. We noticed the red markers fading, but with the help of our map app, we quickly found the original path again. Soon, we reached the Steiglpass at 2,018 meters, where we were rewarded with a stunning view. As predicted, we could also see our hut from afar. Adhit took more photos while I enjoyed the view, posing as a model.

The path to the hut included another descent, but nothing could stop us now. We arrived at Hofpürglhütte at 1,703 meters around 7:30 p.m. and treated ourselves to pasta with tomato sauce. Adhit was happy to have a new bracelet and make friends from Nepal.

## Day 2 – Hiking Through Changing Landscapes

> "The distant mountains are reflected in the eyes of the dragonfly" – Kobayashi Issa

After a good night’s sleep, we started the day. First, we descended into the valley, where we enjoyed our homemade breakfast by a stream. Adhit took the opportunity to wash his shirt, and of course, more photos were taken. We then reached Almsee, where we paused again. There, we met many retirees taking a stroll around the small lake. We stood out with our large backpacks.

After a stop at the chapel, we began another ascent. Another viewpoint provided an opportunity for a break, and Adhit captured the landscape with his camera.

At the Sulzenalmen, we encountered cows near a car, which especially delighted Adhit. Meanwhile, dark clouds gathered above us, which we watched cautiously. At the top of the mountain, at 1,824 meters, we were warned in an Austrian accent: "Take out your shampoo now." After asking twice, I finally understood the sentence and its meaning. We hurried down the mountain to escape the possible rain. Along the way, we met a Czech couple, whom we passed back and forth several times.

While resting on a bench, Adhit and I marveled at how different the world looks upside down. What fun it is to change perspectives! Our joy even attracted cows, but they peacefully passed us by, as did other animals like sheep, goats, and horses we encountered along our journey.

The Austriahütte wasn’t far now, and just before it started raining, we reached our accommodation at 1,638 meters. After a four-minute shower and a longer break, the rain cleared, and we enjoyed dumplings and pancake soup on the terrace. In the evening, Adhit and I watched the sunset, unaware of the next adventure awaiting us.

## Day 3 – Adventure Ahead

> "Over every mountain is a path, although it may not be seen from the valley" – Theodore Roethke

As a challenging day awaited us, Adhit and I were among the first to leave the hut. After just a few minutes, our early start paid off, as we witnessed the rising sun and its rays falling on the trees. Naturally, this was an invitation for the camera, and Adhit beamed with happiness, saying, "In this moment, I am the happiest man alive." With these snapshots, we began our ascent.

The rocky terrain made the climb demanding. But Adhit and I supported each other and were once again rewarded with breakfast and a stunning view. We continued upwards through Edelgrieß. We weren’t alone, though – we met a group of Hungarians from Miskolc, who showed us the best paths. Using our hands and feet, we eventually reached the Edelgrieß Glacier, where we even found a small patch of snow – a pleasant surprise.

The final climb ahead seemed insurmountable at first, but Adhit and I conquered it ("You are my rockstar"), and soon we found ourselves at an incredible 2,532 meters. The view left us speechless. Before us lay a landscape of stones, rocks, and snow – it reminded us of a deserted lunar surface, practically a stone desert. With adrenaline still pumping through our legs, we continued our journey. We walked nearly 4 km through this striking landscape. We saw only a few other hikers, but none were as well-prepared as we were.

Finally, the trail markers indicated our destination, and feeling almost weightless, we looked forward to reaching it and stepping on solid ground. After passing Gruber Scharte, the descent began, through grassy fields, which I embraced warmly. Then, hidden around a corner, Gutenberghaus came into view. What a day!

We immediately took off our hiking boots, slipped into slippers, and ordered hearty hut food – this time, liver loaf with fried potatoes. After a quick wash, we went outside to enjoy the view at 2,146 meters, overlooking the village of Ramstein am Dachstein. Adhit, once again with his professional camera and friendly nature, caught the attention of others, leading to another impromptu photo shoot. A yoga instructor showed us her poses, and we happily became part of the program.

Over goulash soup and Kaiserschmarrn, we met another hiker who entertained us with stories about Dachstein. After paying our bill, the farewell was short – we encountered the same man again in our designated bed in the shared sleeping area. Lucky for us, it was almost like having a private room. Adhit spent the evening taking more photos, and in the morning, he continued from the same spot.

## Day 4 – Outlooks

So far, we had been following a recommended tour, but the next hut wasn’t available, so we decided to spend the extra time in Salzburg. But first, we had to tackle the descent, which involved over 1,000 meters of elevation. Contrary to our plans, we discovered by chance that our train was leaving a day earlier than expected. We hurried down the valley, though a grazing flock of sheep, fascinated by Adhit, delayed our journey.

We barely saved our breakfast. On a bench, we once again found a beautiful view and enjoyed what would be our last breakfast in the mountains.

Though the houses seemed close, the descent was longer than expected. Adhit was dealing with foot pain, but a blister plaster and a change of shoes worked wonders. On the way down, we met a cute dachshund, his owner, and a wedding couple who made village life sound appealing. Even though the blue-violet longhorn beetle we spotted was no longer alive.

## Return Journey

From Kulm, we caught a ride to the train station, where we boarded the train to Salzburg. Adhit had one last goal: to try a döner in Austria, which we promptly did. Smiling at the bus display that read "SAD Austria," we boarded the train to Munich. After another stop and stocking up on supplies, we took the train back to Hamburg, to the flatlands. Thankfully, we had a compartment to ourselves, so we could each enjoy our own activities in peace – Adhit editing photos, and me writing this travel report...
